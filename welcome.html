Athletes first. Families in the corner.

More connection.
Clearer boundaries.

Today, team life happens on phones as well as on the mat. Parents deserve a meaningful place in their athlete’s sporting life, and athletes deserve communication that respects their privacy and the adults responsible for them.

The Wrestling Manager brings team updates, family permissions and covered-conversation review into the same team workspace—helping families stay involved throughout the season.

Parents have a say.

Guardian controls distinguish profile sharing, messaging and health permissions. Covered profile changes follow the applicable approval workflow, giving parents a role in how their athlete is represented and contacted.

Visibility for covered conversations.

Parent mirroring and assigned adult review support oversight of covered communications. Eligible reviewers must be assigned and accept their responsibility; their role does not open unrelated athletes’ conversations or private care records.

Quiet review. Meaningful attention.

Quiet adult review and guardian alert preferences help families manage routine notifications while keeping covered conversations available through authorized access. Alert preferences do not replace active adult supervision.

Private information stays scoped.

Weight history is shared through authorized athlete, family and staff access. Private trainer concerns use separate care permissions; a coaching, reviewer or organization role alone does not unlock private clinical notes.

A clearer view of the season.

Schedules, team announcements and authorized athlete records help families follow what is happening and support their athlete. Less information scattered across team tools makes everyday coordination easier.

Access follows responsibility.

Joining a team or purchasing coverage does not grant permission to see every child’s information. Team, guardian, coaching and care roles have different boundaries, so the right people can support the athlete.

Supporting safer sport communication.

The U.S. Center for SafeSport’s Minor Athlete Abuse Prevention Policies (MAAPP) call for open and transparent adult-to-minor electronic communications, subject to applicable exceptions. Families and teams should review their governing organization’s policy and make sure the required adult is included.

Our family and adult-review tools are designed to support responsible communication practices. Quiet review by itself does not establish MAAPP compliance. The Wrestling Manager is not certified, approved or endorsed by the U.S. Center for SafeSport; technology does not replace training, supervision or reporting responsibilities.

Before you step in

A few things to know.

How do I start using Wrestling Manager?

Open the web app to sign in or create an account. Join through your team’s invitation or code, then use the tools available to your assigned role.

Can I download it from the App Store?

The Apple app is currently in TestFlight. A public App Store link will be added after approval. Contact support about testing.

Does every feature work offline?

Offline support varies by feature and device. Check whether work is saved locally, pending sync or submitted; reconnect for private care and reviewer views. See support information for current limits.

Are tournament tracking and remote reporting available?

Tournament integrations and multi-club remote weekly weigh-in reporting are future development work. They are not advertised here as ready-to-use services.

Who built Wrestling Manager?

Damon Mele, a Wyoming wrestling coach and wrestling organizer, founded Mele Sports Technologies LLC to build tools around the everyday needs of teams, athletes and families. Wrestling Manager is the wrestling product within The Team Manager.
